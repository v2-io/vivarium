# A same-level tile seam carries two objects, and a Jacobi halo at a declared depth and cadence supplies one of them

Two tiles of one nomos at one level are neighbours across a seam that owes them a **boundary datum** (where downhill is) and a **flux** (how much water and sediment crosses). A halo exchanged simultaneously from a frozen snapshot, at a declared depth $d$ and cadence $\sigma$, supplies the datum with a complete key and no dependency payload. The flux is a separate object and is not supplied by a halo.

## Formal Expression

1. **Two objects, and the seam owes both.** At a same-level seam the neighbour relationship has two independent contents:

   - **The boundary datum** — the bed elevation just outside the domain, which fixes the local gradient and therefore where flow leaves. Getting it wrong is what makes a tile grade to its own perimeter and manufactures seam pits ( #obs-tile-outlets-grade-away-the-basins FE(5)).
   - **The boundary flux** — the discharge and sediment entering across the seam from a catchment that lies outside the domain. Getting it wrong is what starves the incision driver by $3.25\times$ (FE(4) of the same segment).

   They fail together and are repaired separately. A halo of depth $d$ carries the datum exactly and carries the flux only from within $d$ cells, so **halo exchange is the datum repair and not the flux repair**; measured, it moves the seam step and the mean elevation onto a single-field carve's values ( #obs-exchange-repairs-the-seam-and-overlap-does-not FE(7)) and leaves the starved driver untouched and unmeasured. Naming the split is what stops the two being paid for once.

2. **The mechanism, stated so it can be built.** A tile of $n^2$ interior cells is carved on an $(n+2d)^2$ window. Every $\sigma$ epochs the interiors of all tiles are assembled into a snapshot; each tile then overwrites its halo cells from that **one frozen snapshot**, falling back to the prior outside the built region, and carries on. Interiors are owned: a tile never writes another tile's cells. The kept output is the $n^2$ interior; the halo is computed and discarded, at a compute cost of $((n+2d)/n)^2$ per unit of kept area, paid every tile and every stage.

3. **Simultaneity is a law obligation, not a preference.** Exchange must be **Jacobi** — every tile reading one snapshot frozen before any tile of the round advances — because #form-depend-by-key-never-latest FE(1) requires two builds along different demand orders to agree bit-for-bit wherever both have materialized. Publishing each tile as it is produced (Gauss–Seidel) makes the world a function of the sweep order, and measured it does so by $130.7\,\mathrm{m}$ mean over $94\,\%$ of a block ( #obs-exchange-repairs-the-seam-and-overlap-does-not FE(8)) — the size of the signal being repaired. A Gauss–Seidel variant is admissible only if its ordering is a **function of tile coordinates** (a red–black colouring, folded into the key as a declared field) and never a function of what the builder happened to schedule; unordered "use the neighbour we already have" is the failure this clause exists to forbid.

4. **The key: a descriptor, not a payload — so the closure is not the planet.** The worry that a neighbour-dependent tile must fold the whole planet into its key conflates two different objects. #form-complete-content-addressed-key FE(1) requires a key to fold everything that *affects the value*; under determinism ( #post-determinism-as-ontology ) folding a **name that determines** an input is exactly as complete as folding that input's bytes. Present practice already relies on this and nothing else: `query::erosion_key` folds $(\text{seed}, \text{face}, \text{level}, o_i, o_j, n_x, \text{epochs})$ plus each dependency's *name and version*, and never the content hash of the initial-topography, uplift or climate tiles it consumes — because those coordinates and versions determine those tiles. The same move carries the halo: the key gains the **schedule descriptor** $(d, \sigma, \text{contract})$, from which the neighbour set is a function of the tile's own coordinates, and the neighbours' states one round back are determined by the same descriptor applied to their coordinates, recursively, terminating at round zero on the prior. **Key payload stays $O(1)$ and stays a pure function of coordinates; nothing needs materializing to compute a key or to ask the store whether it holds one.**

5. **The cone is real, and it is a scheduling cost rather than a keying one.** What the recursion *does* imply is a dependency cone: because a tile's whole interior is coupled within one round (Priority-Flood and accumulation are global inside the domain), one round of exchange spreads influence one tile outward, so building a single tile at round $N$ from cold materializes a neighbourhood of radius $N$ tiles — $\Theta(N^3)$ tile-rounds for one isolated tile. A builder **sweeping a region** pays nothing extra: every tile-round is shared, and the total is $N \times$ (tiles in the region). **The cone is the price of laziness, not of the design** — which places the cost exactly on demand-driven single-tile pulls deep in a chain, the beacon's own access pattern, and exactly not on the region builds the planet is made of. A declared **cone truncation radius** $\rho$ (beyond $\rho$ tiles, halo values fall back to the prior; $\rho$ in the key) is the lever that restores laziness at a declared cost, and its cost is unmeasured — the first measurement this design owes.

6. **What is declared is an approximation with a measured budget.** A finite $(d, \sigma, \rho)$ is a declared deficiency, not a hidden one ( #norm-declared-violation-is-not-license ), and its budget is now data rather than doctrine ( #disc-unlawfulness-budget FE(3)): at the beacon grain, $d=16$, $\sigma=10$ leaves the block's mean elevation $4.8\,\mathrm{m}$ from a single-field carve where the shipped tiling is $36.2\,\mathrm{m}$ from it, at $2.25\times$ compute. The budget is stated in **structural** quantities and may not be stated in pointwise ones: a $1\,\mathrm{mm}$ perturbation $96$ cells away decorrelates $96.6\,\%$ of a core in $300$ epochs ( #obs-exchange-repairs-the-seam-and-overlap-does-not FE(2)), so cell-by-cell agreement with a single-field carve is not available to any method and must not appear in any promise.

7. **The cadence is the stage stride, and that forces it into the key.** The carrier already exists: #form-time-indexed-stage-chains materializes a settle history as keyed rungs every `stage_stride` epochs, and `query::erosion_tile_staged` documents that the stride is **demand, not identity** — a rung at $k$ epochs holds byte-identical heights whether built as a chain or one-shot, convicted by `staged_chain_is_bit_identical_to_one_shot`, with its own note that *if that test ever breaks, the stride has become identity and must move into the key*. Exchange at cadence $\sigma$ breaks it by construction: a tile that imports its neighbours every $\sigma$ epochs computes a different bed for different $\sigma$. So the design's first consequence for the existing chain is precise and checkable — **$\sigma$ is identity and enters the key**, and the exchange cadence and the stage stride should be the same number rather than two schedules that would silently compose.

8. **Same-level seam ≠ hanging node, and only one of them generalizes across level.** #form-face-flux-register governs a **non-matching** interface where a coarse face abuts finer sub-faces, and its object is a single-valued face flux. This segment governs a **matching** interface between equals, and its object is the datum. Aiming the face register at the same-level base-level problem does not fit: there is no hanging node, nothing is subdivided, and the defect is a wrong elevation rather than a double-counted flux. The direction that *does* generalize is the flux half of FE(1): a flux record can be length-weighted down to any grain ( #form-face-flux-register FE(3)) and summed over a stage chain to any interval, whereas a halo of raw **states** has no honest restriction across level ( #obs-mean-pin-manufactures-seam convicts the obvious one). So the level-asymmetric case — a tile whose neighbours are resolved finer, and whose edges therefore carry a *history* of fluxes at a finer grain — is a **flux-record** problem and not a halo problem, and it is out of scope here rather than solved here.

9. **A restored fill puts a *non-local* datum on the seam, and no halo depth supplies it.** Once the epoch returns its depression fill to the bed ( #obs-lakes-are-routed-over-not-carved-away FE(1)), a stored bed holds closed basins, and routing is derived on the **spill surface** rather than on the bed. A basin that straddles a seam therefore hands its neighbours a quantity the halo cannot carry: its **spill level** is a property of the whole basin — the lowest point on its entire rim — and a halo of depth $d$ truncates the basin at $d$ cells. Two tiles can then assign different spill levels to one lake and route its outflow in different directions, and **the disagreement does not decay with $d$**, because a minimum over a set does not decay as the set is enlarged; it jumps when the true rim low enters the window and is simply wrong until then.

   This is the first quantity met here for which the honest dependency footprint is #detail-drainage-dependency-planning FE(1)'s **drainage island** rather than a ring — and it is the reason that segment's "not a Euclidean halo" is a claim about basins and not a stylistic preference. So the boundary datum of FE(1) resolves into two parts: a **local** part (the bed elevation just outside the domain, which the halo supplies exactly) and a **non-local** part (the spill level of any basin crossing the seam, which it does not). The candidate for the second is a **per-basin scalar exchanged beside the halo** — one number per straddling basin, which is a sufficient statistic in #form-seam-flux-exchange FE(2)'s sense and would key like a stage residual does ( #form-time-indexed-stage-chains FE(3)). It is unbuilt, unmeasured, and named here rather than assumed away.

   The measurements this design rests on were taken under the restoring law, so straddling lakes were present in every arm and the exchange still put the seam step and the mean elevation on a single-field carve's values ( #obs-exchange-repairs-the-seam-and-overlap-does-not FE(7)). What that does **not** establish is agreement on the lakes themselves: no lake-specific statistic discriminates in that probe, so the size of this defect is unknown rather than small.

10. **Out of bounds.** (a) One-shot overlap sold as the cheap repair — it reads no neighbour, so its key is free, and it is measured worse than doing nothing ( #obs-exchange-repairs-the-seam-and-overlap-does-not FE(6)). (b) Any promise of pointwise agreement with a single-field carve. (c) Exchange whose ordering depends on the builder's schedule. (d) Claiming this repairs the incision driver. (e) Treating a declared $(d, \sigma, \rho)$ as the boundary contract's *right* answer rather than as one entry in the declaration column #form-declared-boundary-contract FE(2) opens.

## Epistemic Status

**Max attainable: exact** for FE(4)'s key-completeness argument, which is a property of content-addressing under determinism and is checkable against `store::Key::with_dep_versions` and `query::erosion_key` by inspection; and for FE(3)'s order-independence obligation, which is #form-depend-by-key-never-latest restated at a new seam and is convicted by probe.

**Currently `robust-qualitative`.** FE(1)'s split, FE(2)'s mechanism, FE(3)'s Jacobi choice and FE(6)'s budget are backed by measurement at one grain, one seed and one footprint ( #obs-exchange-repairs-the-seam-and-overlap-does-not ), which is enough to convict the direction and not enough to fix $d$, $\sigma$ or $\rho$ as law. FE(5)'s cone arithmetic is **derived from the mechanism and not measured**: the claim that one round spreads influence exactly one tile follows from the fill and accumulation being global within a domain, and no probe has yet built a chain deep enough to observe the cone. FE(7) is exact as a statement about the existing test's own stated condition on the *default* (no-exchange) path — `staged_chain_is_bit_identical_to_one_shot` still convicts demand-not-identity there; under exchange, $\sigma$ is identity and already enters the key via `HaloSchedule`. FE(8) is argued, not measured.

**Production path built (2026-07-29).** `HaloSchedule { depth, cadence, cone_rho }` is a keyable descriptor; `query::erosion_key` folds `edge=halo|d|sigma|rho` when a schedule is present and leaves the historical key shape when it is not; `erosion::carve_region_jacobi_exchange` is the library form of the probe's Jacobi arm (with per-rung hooks); `World::erosion_region_exchanged_staged` memos each interior under that complete key; **`vivarium build` erodes each face (and the beacon) as a Jacobi region** via `HaloSchedule::for_build` (cadence = stage stride); loaders and `erosion_tile`'s store half **prefer halo over plain** at the same coordinates and epochs. Tripwires: Jacobi determinism, schedule keys do not collide with plain tiling, exchanged interiors are not a bit-alias of edge-sink tiles, load prefers halo. The beacon FE(7) structural table remains the probe's job at its measured grain — unit tests do not re-litigate it.

**Still open.** Flux half of FE(1); spill-level scalar (FE(9)); single-tile demand cone with measured $\rho$; **declared** cube-edge resampling for $d \ge 2$ (production currently **clamps** — #obs-chart-edge-halo-clamps-to-the-face ); operating $(d,\sigma,\rho)$ not Joseph-ratified as law (`for_build` uses measured $d{=}16$ and stride-as-$\sigma$). Companion DECISIONS remain `:by claude :status proposed`. Stage `draft`.

**Scope: measured within one face; cube edges are a separate seam family.** The backing probe ran entirely interior to a face (L13, 4×4 tiles), and its $d{=}16$ budgets inherit that scope. Across a cube edge the two grids co-align **only on the shared edge**: depth-1 exact; depth 2+ mismatches (PROBE 2 — #form-cellid-chunk-patch FE(4)). Production Jacobi on whole faces made the edge load-bearing: multi-cell off-chart samples **clamp to the face chart** rather than resample ( #obs-chart-edge-halo-clamps-to-the-face ). That clamp is a chart-edge boundary contract in force, not a synonym of same-level tile exchange ( #form-seam-families ). Resampling must eventually enter the key beside $(d, \sigma, \rho)$.

**Store identity on adoption.** When both a plain edge-sink memo and a halo-schedule memo exist at the same `(face, level, oi, oj, nx, epochs)`, loaders and `erosion_tile`'s store half **prefer the halo article** — a store-cohort rule ( #form-seam-families ), not a physics claim. Plain keys remain creatable for instruments.

**The falsifier that would retire this design rather than tune it:** an exchange arm at any $(d, \sigma)$ that does not bring the block's structural statistics onto a single-field carve's, or a demonstration that the datum and the flux are not separable because supplying the datum alone changes the drainage network enough to invalidate the flux comparison. The first is measured false at one grain; the second is untested.

## Discussion

The design's shape was set by a negative result. The attractive version of a halo reads no neighbour at all — carve wider, keep the middle — and it is attractive precisely because it costs nothing in the currency this project is most careful about: the key stays a pure function of coordinates, no cone exists, and build order cannot matter. That version is measured worse than doing nothing. What it removes is the perimeter flattening that was making the shipped seam quiet, and quiet is not small.

Having to pay for the dependency after all is what made the key question worth answering properly, and the answer is that the bill is much smaller than it looks: a complete key has never contained its inputs, only names that determine them, so a neighbour-dependent tile folds in a schedule descriptor and stays $O(1)$. The whole-planet closure was an artefact of imagining the key as a payload. What survives the correction is the cone — a real cost, in the right place, falling on lazy single-tile pulls and not on region builds.

The two-object split in FE(1) is the part most likely to matter beyond erosion. A seam between equals owes a datum and a flux; a seam between grains owes a single-valued flux and nothing else. Reading the same-level problem through the hanging-node register conflates them, and the conflation is easy because both are called seams and both are governed by one law ( #form-seam-flux-exchange ). One law, two objects, two repairs.
