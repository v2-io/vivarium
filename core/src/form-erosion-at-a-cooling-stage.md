# Erosion at a cooling stage: the fluvial rung gains a $T_p$ coordinate, and the composition forks on physics rather than on cost

An eroded bed carries the mantle potential temperature of the surface it carved in its complete key, which makes the fluvial chain the first materialized-only chain composed over a law-evaluable one — and the choice between carving each cooling stage independently and carrying a bed forward between stages is not a cost trade, because the second option occupies the ground the pour-grain rock-mass ledger already stands on.

## Formal Expression

1. **The coordinate, and the word for it.** The `erosion-tile` nomos today takes $(\mathrm{seed}, \mathrm{face}, \mathrm{level}, o_i, o_j, n_x, \mathrm{epochs})$ and carves the surface at the present anchor, because its seed surface is `gen::initial_topography_m` and that is `tectonic_surface_m` at `MANTLE_TP_C` ( #detail-erosion-composition FE(4)). Parameterizing it adds one coordinate: the **cooling stage**, identified by its mantle potential temperature $T_p$. No new time-noun is coined. `LEXICON` already reserves **epoch** for the erosion solver's step, **phase** for a span of the ordinum ladder, and #form-time-indexed-stage-chains uses **stage** for a keyed rung of a chain; a fourth word naming an object that already has a name would be a fork, and the 2026-07-24 `epoch`/`epoch` collision is the local evidence for what that costs.

2. **The stage rides in the key as $T_p$ bits, never as a stage index.** The `epoch-reduction` aspect already keys on `tp_bits` — the `f64` bit pattern, so two stages never alias ( #form-complete-content-addressed-key ) — and the erosion key takes the same field for the same reason. An **index** into the chain would be under-keying: #form-time-indexed-stage-chains FE(9) makes the chain nested by bisection, so stage $i$ of refinement $k$ is stage $2i$ of refinement $k{+}1$, and an index is therefore a function of the requested density rather than of the world. Two builds at different densities would collide on index and serve each other's terrain.

   **This rekeys the tile layer once.** Every artifact whose value depends on the stage — initial topography, uplift, erosion, water — takes the field, so every existing tile memo becomes a miss. That is the over-key-never-under-key trade taken deliberately ( #form-complete-content-addressed-key FE(2)), and its practical size is bounded by the fact that any edit under `crates/vivarium-world/src/` already invalidates the store through `SRC_HASH` (FE(4) of the same segment).

3. **This is a materialized-only chain composed over a law-evaluable one, and that composition is new.** #form-time-indexed-stage-chains FE(8) separates chains whose stages are pure functions of a time-index (the cooling chain: $T_p(t)$ is closed form, so a stage nobody built can still be evaluated) from chains whose stages exist because something ran (the erosion chain). Erosion at a cooling stage is the first object indexed by **both**. Its consequences are the ones FE(8) already assigns to each kind, applied one axis at a time: the $T_p$ axis may be sampled at any density a consumer likes and costs nothing to refine, while the epoch axis has exactly the density that was built and refining it is a build request.

4. **The two axes are one clock, and whether they are consistent is what the composition forks on.** Both $T_p$ and epochs are world-time: #obs-the-erodible-world-is-down-the-cooling-chain FE(8) measures the exchange rate the tree currently declares (353 epochs per $0.1$ Ga stage gap at $\delta = 0.05$ °C/epoch, 3530 for the canonical chain). Two compositions are available and they differ in whether the epoch axis actually advances the $T_p$ axis.

   **(A) Stage-parallel.** $\mathrm{bed}(T_p) = \mathrm{erode}\big(\mathrm{surface}(T_p),\, E\big)$ — each stage carved independently from its own column surface for the same authored epoch count. No history between stages. Every stage is a pure function of $(\mathrm{seed}, \mathrm{cell}, T_p, E)$, so stages may be built in any order, in parallel, and omitted freely.

   **(B) Chain-carried.** $\mathrm{bed}(T_{p,n}) = \mathrm{erode}\big(\mathrm{bed}(T_{p,n-1}) + \Delta\mathrm{column}(n{-}1 \to n),\, E\big)$ — the bed persists and the column's change between stages is the uplift applied to it. This is the iterated erode → rebound → re-erode chain #form-isostasy-column FE(9) names as its own open rung, and the increment it adds is exactly the quantity `uplift.rs` already computes, integrated over a stage gap instead of one epoch.

5. **(B) is not a rung beside the rock-mass ledger; it is a candidate replacement for it.** The live default surface is post-ledger: at every $T_p$, `erosion_return` debits crust by a relief-driven law, rebounds the column, and re-pours ( #form-isostasy-column FE(9)). That debit is one erosion maturity step at $\varphi = 1$, and it is **large** — a mean of 76 m of removed stand at the present anchor rising to 491 m by 1.0 Ga, against a subaerial relief standard deviation of 347–563 m ( #obs-the-erodible-world-is-down-the-cooling-chain FE(6)). A fluvial bed carried across stages under (B), on surfaces from which a stage-dependent maturity step has already been subtracted, would be carving erosion that the pour grain has already applied. So (B) has a precondition (A) does not: it must either drive from the **pre-ledger** column (`tectonic_surface_pre_ledger_at_tp`, which is what `uplift.rs` already differences) and take over the ledger's role as the mass-return path, or leave the ledger in place and accept a declared double count. Choosing (B) is therefore a decision about #form-isostasy-column FE(9), not only about erosion.

6. **Under (A) the cooling stage joins the cohort; under (B) it joins the time-index.** #form-time-indexed-stage-chains FE(11) makes a chain coherent only within one cohort identified by $(\mathrm{src}, \mathrm{level})$, on the grounds that ordering artifacts from two different worlds on one time axis draws their difference as elapsed world-time. Under (A) the stage is exactly such a field: two beds at different $T_p$ are two independent carvings, and scrubbing epochs across them would paint the column's evolution as fluvial work, so the reader's selector becomes $(\mathrm{src}, \mathrm{level}, T_p, \mathrm{epochs})$ with the first three held fixed. Under (B) the pair $(T_p, \mathrm{epochs})$ *is* the time-index, monotone along the walk, and a scrub across stages is the world evolving — which is the picture the ladder wants, and is available only from the composition that pays for it.

7. **Which stages get carved is demand; which stage a bed carved is identity.** The membership test #form-manifest-prescribes-vivium FE(5) states is whether the artifact's bytes depend on the field. A bed at $(T_p, \mathrm{epochs})$ holds the same bytes however many other stages were requested, so the **set** of stages a world carves belongs in the demand bucket beside `frames` and `erosion_epochs`, and the individual $T_p$ belongs in the key (FE(2)). Under (A) that is the whole answer, and the default is the present anchor — which keeps every manifest written before the field existed opening as the world it always was ( `spec.rs` already holds that property for the demand bucket). Under (B) the set is not free: a carried bed depends on its predecessor by complete key ( #form-depend-by-key-never-latest ), so demand may choose where a walk *stops*, never which interior stages are skipped.

8. **The stage's own waterline travels with it.** `Fluvial::outlets` reaches `sea_level::derived_sea_level_m(seed)` — the present anchor's waterline, with no stage argument — and so do two further reads inside the kernel's epoch loop ( #detail-erosion-composition FE(4)). A stage-parameterized erosion supplies `derived_sea_level_at_tp` for its own $T_p$. The magnitude of getting this wrong is **small and was measured rather than assumed**: 0.0–0.62 % of cells change land/sea class across every stage and seed ( #obs-the-erodible-world-is-down-the-cooling-chain FE(7)), because derived sea moves ~150 m across the chain while relief is several hundred. It is a correctness item located at the coast, not a large source of error, and saying so is part of the claim.

9. **Retention has a bound only under (B), and the bound is a selection policy.** Under (A) nothing is retained between stages: each is recomputable from $(\mathrm{seed}, T_p)$ alone, so a world may hold any subset of stages and discard any of them. Under (B) the walk is path-dependent and the question becomes which interior stages to keep under a storage budget — a problem with a rigorous form and a large literature reachable through `msc/research-lem-sota/lem-time-interior-prior-art-2026-07-28.md` §3.2, whose transferable result is that optimal placement is non-uniform, and whose transferable constraint is sharper than the result: **any such ladder is computed as a selection policy over stages that remain addressed by their own $T_p$ and epoch count**, never as an alternative addressing scheme, because a boundary set that entered identity would cost FE(9)'s subset property.

10. **Recommended first build: one cold stage, stage-parallel, at the whole-world level.** (A) at a single $T_p$ near the canonical cold end is a strict prefix of everything above: it needs the key field (FE(2)), the surface and waterline threading (FE(8)), and a demand field with the present anchor as default (FE(7)) — and it needs no decision about the ledger, no retention policy, and no change to the cohort reader beyond one more selector field. It answers the question the ladder actually asks (*what does a carved cold world look like?*), it is refutable by looking at it, and #obs-the-erodible-world-is-down-the-cooling-chain FE(3) says there is no colder frontier to chase afterwards. Its declared deficiency is stated up front and is not small: the bed is $E$ epochs of dissection on a cold column, **not** the landscape that walked there, and it must not be described as the latter.

11. **Out of bounds for this segment.** Declaring which $T_p$ the default world should carve; choosing between (A) and (B) as project law — this segment states what separates them and what each costs, and #form-isostasy-column FE(9) owns the half of that choice which is about the ledger; specifying the store schema for a stage-parameterized artifact; any claim that either composition is built; and the calibration of $\delta$, which #obs-the-erodible-world-is-down-the-cooling-chain FE(8) shows is a joint question with `epoch ↔ years` rather than this segment's to settle.

## Epistemic Status

**Max attainable: exact** for FE(2) and FE(7) — the keying argument follows from #form-complete-content-addressed-key plus the nesting property, and the demand/identity split follows from #form-manifest-prescribes-vivium FE(5)'s stated membership test — once a build exists for a test to convict. **Empirical** for FE(5) and FE(8), which rest on measurements homed at #obs-the-erodible-world-is-down-the-cooling-chain rather than derived here. **Formulation** throughout for the (A)/(B) fork: both fit the priors, and FE(10) is a recommendation with reasons, not a derivation.

**Currently `sketch`.** Nothing here is built. The design is stated at the grain a builder could act on and at no finer grain, and its central claim — that (A) and (B) differ on physics rather than on cost — is the one most worth attacking, because the whole ordering of the work follows from it. Stage `draft`; **no Joseph DECISIONS row**, and the accompanying entry is `:by claude :status proposed`. Do not cite as ratified.

**The falsifier.** If a fluvial bed carried under (B) on the pre-ledger column reproduced the post-ledger surface's stand statistics to within the ledger's own crudeness, FE(5)'s "candidate replacement" would be understated and the two would be the same law at two grains rather than rivals; if it diverged badly, the ledger's $\varphi = 1$ step is doing something the fluvial kernel does not, and FE(5) would be understating the conflict instead. Either outcome is reachable by one build and neither has been attempted.

## Discussion

The reason this looked like a scheduling question and is not: at a single cooling stage, a maturity-step ledger and a fluvial kernel are two ways of removing rock from the same column, and nothing distinguishes them because there is no second moment at which to check whether they agree. A chain supplies that second moment. Once the bed persists across stages, the ledger's per-stage debit and the fluvial history stop being independent accounts of erosion and start being two entries for it — so the composition that makes the world's history visible is the same composition that makes the double count visible, and neither is available without the other.

The cheaper option is not therefore a compromise. Carving one cold stage answers what the ladder wanted to know, costs about what has already been paid ( #obs-the-erodible-world-is-down-the-cooling-chain Working Notes), and leaves the ledger question exactly where #form-isostasy-column FE(9) already put it, unprejudiced. What it cannot do is show a landscape with a past, and the honest form of that limitation is a sentence in the segment rather than a caveat beside a screenshot.

## Working Notes

- **Cross-tile base level composes with this and the order matters.** #obs-tile-outlets-grade-away-the-basins FE(4)–(5) caps every basin at one tile; repairing it makes a bed depend on its neighbours. Under (A) that is confined within a stage and the two repairs are independent. Under (B) it is not: stage $n$'s bed would depend on neighbouring tiles' stage $n{-}1$ beds, which is a dependency footprint the current per-tile key does not express, and a cross-tile repair landing after (B) would rekey the whole walk rather than one rung. If both are wanted, the base-level repair is cheaper first.
- **Unbuilt and worth one measurement before either option:** a timed cold-stage sweep. FE(10)'s cost claim rests on an inference (the kernel runs its full composition on subaerially empty tiles, so cost is roughly flat in land fraction) from a premise nobody has measured in seconds.
- **Do not re-open:** a stage index as the key field (FE(2) — nesting makes it density-dependent); a per-stage retention ladder that changes how a stage is addressed (FE(9)); coining a fourth time-word for the cooling stage (FE(1)).
- Sibling: #obs-the-erodible-world-is-down-the-cooling-chain (the measurements); #form-isostasy-column FE(9) (the ledger half of the (B) decision); #form-time-indexed-stage-chains FE(8)/(11) (the chain kinds and the cohort this extends).
