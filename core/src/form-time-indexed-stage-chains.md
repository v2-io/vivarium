# Time-indexed stage chains: a settle sequence is addressable memos, each carrying its own $\varepsilon$

A world does not reach a state, it walks to one. That walk is a chain of keyed nomos invocations, each carrying a **time-index in its complete key** and recording the **convergence-$\varepsilon$** it actually reached — so a stage is a thing you can point at, resume from, watch, and replay, rather than a number of iterations someone chose.

## Formal Expression

1. **The unit.** A settle sequence is not one long computation with a step count. It is a **chain of stages**, each an independently keyed memo whose complete key includes a **time-index** ( #form-complete-content-addressed-key ; the time component named at #form-three-scoped-runtime FE(3)). Stage $n{+}1$ depends on stage $n$ **by key**, never by "the latest state on disk" ( #form-depend-by-key-never-latest ).

2. **Addressability is the point, and the tree's interior is measured.** With a time-index in the key, *"the beginning of the third fluvial stage"* is a **key**, not a description. That is what a beacon can aim at, what a watchpoint can name, and what a builder can resume from. Without it, a build has no interior: it is opaque between "started" and "finished," and the only addressable states are the endpoints.

   This is countable rather than merely assertable, and `watch::interior` counts it — distinct time-index values per nomos in a store. On a completed L9 build (2026-07-28): **`mantle-thermal` 81, `erosion-tile` 8 fresh (epochs 5–40 at the manifest stride; the census reports 9 across all build history, the ninth a stale-source cohort at `epochs=60`), `water-tile` 1.** Two chains in the tree now have an interior; **water is the only relaxation kernel with two ends and nothing between**. Two readings of that census are wrong in ways that flatter it, so both are pinned by tests: water's index is `steps`, **not** the `eepochs` naming which eroded bed it settles onto (a dependency selector, #form-depend-by-key-never-latest ), and the cooling reductions key under `mantle-thermal` with `aspect=epoch-reduction` rather than under a nomos of that name.

3. **Each stage records the $\varepsilon$ it reached.** A stage's memo carries not only its result but the convergence residual it achieved and the criterion it was run against. That number is not diagnostics — it is **the unLawfulness budget as data** ( #disc-unlawfulness-budget FE(3): every Realized freeze injects bounded-but-nonzero residual drift, and downstream honesty gates on the *cumulative* budget). An $\varepsilon$ that is never recorded is an unLawfulness budget asserted to be zero.

   First live instance: every computed erosion stage records its final-epoch mean $|\Delta h|$ as a keyed sibling (`aspect=stage-residual`, one f32; reader `World::erosion_stage_residual`). This is the **measured-residual half only** — a number recorded, not a criterion certified: erosion has no earnable convergence criterion ( #obs-erosion-residual-is-driver-bound ), so the record says what the kernel *did* and certifies nothing. The sibling-artifact shape is provisional until a second kernel records one.

4. **A criterion replaces an iteration count — and the count becomes a measurement.** A relaxation-to-attractor process ( #detail-phenomena-systems-map FE(6) approach **R**) has no natural step count; it has a **declared criterion**. The criterion is declared on the nomos and folded into its key; the steps actually taken are then an *output* a probe can convict ("did it converge, and in how many"). An authored step count is a number nothing can falsify, and it silently changes meaning whenever the kernel or the terrain does. Three `ASSUMPTIONS.md` rows currently marked **arbitrary** name this mechanism as their cure: `epoch ↔ years` ("component E must pin it"), erosion run length, and water fill steps. Two live `NomosDecl` `status` strings declare the same absence.

   **The criterion is not always a residual tolerance, and assuming it is has now failed twice under measurement.** Both live relaxation kernels turn out to have no near-stationarity to detect — water because its timestep is pinned below the scale at which anything settles ( #obs-water-fill-never-settles ), erosion because sustained uplift pins its residual at the driver's rate and most tiles do no fluvial work at all ( #obs-erosion-residual-is-driver-bound ). The clause holds; the *form* of the criterion is per-kernel and has to be earned by measuring what the kernel actually approaches. **Declaring an arbitrary count on the nomos does not discharge this** — relocating an unjustified number from a CLI flag into the declaration layer makes it look principled without making it convictable, which is strictly worse than leaving it where it visibly is an expedient.

5. **Replay and live-watching are one mechanism, not two features.** An explorer watching a build progress and an explorer scrubbing a finished world's history are both **reading a chain of time-indexed memos**. They differ only in whether new indices are still landing. Building them as two features produces two, and they will disagree. The reader is a poller over roots; the store is already the bus ( #form-store-as-save , #detail-builder-daemon FE(1)).

   Built as `vivarium watch` (`watch.rs`): live and `--replay` share one census type and one render call, and differ only in where the next census comes from. The reader takes no lock and writes nothing, so it cannot disturb a builder ( #form-builder-admission ). **What it can honestly order by is root *landing* time — build history, not world-time**, because root files carry no world-time; the command states this on screen rather than letting the animation imply otherwise. FE(2)'s interior census is the measurement of that gap, and closing it is what a recorded time-index per stage would buy.

6. **Time-uniform where the aspect demands it.** Stage chains do not make every aspect demand-shaped. Regime G aspects — tectonics, sea level, the mantle-thermal trajectory — advance **time-uniformly** because a planet-spanning object cannot be held half in the past ( #form-temporal-lod-regimes FE(2)). For those, the chain *is* the spine and its density is set by what a consumer must resolve, not by a placed beacon. Regime H (history-dependent local) is where a chain is walked on demand as catch-up.

7. **Retention is bounded, and the bound has a primary.** A chain does not require keeping every stage. Gear–Wells order integration **slowest-component-first** precisely so a failed step needs only "reduce the last step," retaining **one prior value per component** — fastest-first would demand unstable reverse integration or prohibitive storage — and power-of-two stepsizes make every slower mesh a subset of every faster one ( #detail-seam-precedents FE(5), primary-read). That is the retention law for stage chains: enough history to back up one coupling step, no more.

8. **Two kinds of chain, and only one of them has a density anyone may choose.** A chain's stages are either **law-evaluable** — each stage a pure function of its time-index, computable at any time-index on demand (the mantle-thermal cooling chain: $T_p(t)$ is closed-form, so a stage nobody built can still be evaluated) — or **materialized-only**, where a stage exists because something *ran* to produce it and does not exist otherwise (an erosion stage chain, once it has one: reaching epoch 37 means integrating to epoch 37). The distinction is not about importance or fidelity; it is about whether the time-index can be *inverted* into a state without paying the history.

   It decides three things that otherwise look like free choices. **Density**: a law-evaluable chain may be sampled as finely as a consumer likes ( #form-core-view-wall FE(5): observation density is not authorship); a materialized-only chain has exactly the density that was built, and asking for more is a *build* request, not a view parameter. **Densification** (FE(9)): bisection refines a law-evaluable chain for free, and cannot refine a materialized-only one at all — an intermediate stage must be *computed*, and for a path-dependent kernel that means re-running from the last retained state. **Cold-start**: a law-evaluable chain has none; a materialized-only chain inherits the cold-start cost in full ( #form-three-scoped-runtime FE(4) ).

   **A consumer must therefore not assume the first kind.** The live instance: `vivarium explore` today reaches `mantle_thermal::cooling_stages_refined` directly, so its whole time axis is one phase's driver, and its sampling-density parameter is meaningful *only* because that chain happens to be law-evaluable. Erosion — now the tree's first materialized-only chain with an interior — has *exactly* the built density (`erosion_stage_stride`), so the same parameter would be meaningless against it. Which nomos supplies a viewer's time axis is a property of the world at its current phase, not knowledge a viewer should carry (Joseph 2026-07-27: *"I don't see any situation where it makes sense for `explore --frames` to have to know that it happens to be something about cooling — especially since that's a very early stage that we'll be leaving behind soon"*).

   The materialized-only kind is live as of 2026-07-28: the erosion settle history lands as keyed stages every `erosion_stage_stride` epochs (manifest demand — #form-manifest-prescribes-vivium FE(5)), stage $k$ seeded from stage $k-s$'s stored heights over a ladder that is a function of `(epochs, stride)` and never of store contents ( #form-depend-by-key-never-latest ). **Density is demand precisely because a stage's bytes are stride-independent**: chained ≡ one-shot bit-exactly, convicted by `query::tests::staged_chain_is_bit_identical_to_one_shot`, whose failure would be the signal that the stride has become identity and must enter the key. The kind's cold-start clause is also its migration path, convicted in the same suite: a world built endpoint-only pays one settle history to gain its interior — the endpoint citizen it already holds is reached through the chain unchanged.

   Three clauses the chain's first instance carries. **The mechanical hinge of stride-independence is that the per-epoch step is a *total-order pure function* of the height field**: every derived object (fill, receivers, drainage, stack order) is recomputed from `h` each epoch — there is no hidden network state to fail to round-trip — and the one place float ties could inject ambience, the Priority-Flood heap, is ordered by `(total_cmp, insertion seq)`, a total order (`erosion.rs`). Purity alone is not rare in this model family; what is distinctive — and load-bearing for the whole time-interior design — is purity *plus* total-order tie-breaking *plus* a test that convicts it (`staged_chain_is_bit_identical_to_one_shot` appears to be the first restart-equivalence conviction in the LEM field; claim the conviction, not the purity). **Cadence must never alter physics** (the converse invariant, from the field's measured failures — display cadence clipping `Δt` is how output settings silently change results in the wild): stage density may *partition* the epoch sequence, and must never change `Δt`, the epoch count, or where uplift is applied. The staged walk honors this by construction (a rung is exactly `erode(k−k_{prev})` on the predecessor's bytes), and the equivalence test convicts any future violation — including the tempting optimization ("coarsen the quiet stages") that is this violation wearing a performance win. **Addressable is accurate only below a stated bound:** Braun & Willett validate the transient below a closed-form timestep limit (2013 Appendix B, "Limit on time step" — at $m{=}0.5$, $\Delta t < C_{\max}\cdot 10^4/n_x$; empirically their transient converges for $\Delta t \le 1000$ y) — the circulating "accurate at 100× the stable timestep" figure is that bound at their parameters with the conditions stripped. An interior stage is a transient state, so whether ours sit inside the bound is *arithmetic*, measured by `Fluvial::response_census` (`examples/response_time_probe`): on the live world's landiest tiles the per-epoch Courant number is p50 ≈ 0.02, max ≈ 0.11 — an order of magnitude inside the bound at L9, tightening ×2 per level finer. The sidebar `msc/research-lem-sota/lem-time-interior-prior-art-2026-07-28.md` carries the literature (and the a-priori response time and χ-shape criterion that answer FE(4)'s open form).

9. **Densification by bisection is what makes a *law-evaluable* chain's density a demand parameter.** A chain sampled more finely is only a *refinement* if the coarse stages survive in it **bit-exactly**: each stage's time-index rides in its complete key ( #form-complete-content-addressed-key ), so a stage that moves by one ULP is not the same stage — it is a new key, and the "refinement" orphans every memo on the chain and recomputes the world. Power-of-two bisection over an exact integer grid gives the subset property FE(7) names, on the time axis rather than the space axis.

   **Measured** (`mantle_thermal::cooling_stages_refined`, 2026-07-24): against a store built at 81 stages, requesting 6, 11, and 21 yields 6, 11, and 21 store **hits**, 0 computed, in microseconds. A requested count that is not a nested count rounds *up* and the builder says so — refusing the request or interpolating to it exactly would each cost the subset property, which is worth more than the round number.

10. **Live partial state.** Two chains have interiors. The mantle-thermal cooling chain (law-evaluable, FE(8)): `query::World::epoch_reduction` keys by `(mantle-thermal identity, seed, tp_bits, chain versions)`, is a store citizen, and is resumable — the round-trip probe convicts its order-independence and its resume-equals-run-through property on exactly that chain ( #form-depend-by-key-never-latest ); its density is a demand parameter (FE(9)); its stages record no $\varepsilon$ and need none — each is an exact law evaluation, not a relaxation. The erosion chain (materialized-only, FE(8)): interior stages at the manifest stride, each recording its measured residual (FE(3)). **No kernel yet runs to a criterion instead of a count** — the erosion residual is recorded honesty, not a gate (FE(4)).

11. **Reading a chain in world-time is a key predicate, and it needs *two* fields, not one.** The world-time reading FE(5) leaves open is not new machinery: it is the same loader under a different predicate over the complete key — hold the time-index fixed and take every tile at it. Landing-order replay asks "which roots existed by wall-clock *t*"; world-time playback asks "which stage of each tile *is* time-index *k*", and the second is answerable precisely because the time-index rides in the key (FE(2)).

    **The other two fields are the constraint, and omitting either produces a picture indistinguishable from geology.** A chain is coherent only within one **cohort**, identified by `(src, level)`, and the reader selects `(src, level, time-index)`.

    **`src`**: two stages carved under different whole-crate digests ( #form-complete-content-addressed-key FE(4)) are stages of *two different worlds*, so ordering them on one time axis draws the difference between two kernels as though it were elapsed world-time. This is the stale-`src` two-datum fault ( #obs-coarse-view-draws-the-uncarved-prior 's sibling failure) relocated onto the time axis, and it is worse there: a mixed *space* datum shows a coverage ribbon a viewer can see, while a mixed *time* datum shows smooth plausible change.

    **`level`**: a world can hold a coarse global sweep and a fine beacon patch at once, with different ladders over different footprints (live, 2026-07-28: global L9 at epochs 5…40 beside an L13 beacon at 10…300). Their ladders overlap but their *coverage* does not, so a merged chain has rungs whose drawn area jumps — and since the coarse ladder runs out first, scrubbing the fine chain past its neighbour's end makes the terrain *around* the region revert to the uncarved prior. That is a coverage cliff, and on screen it is a landscape changing. **This half was found by looking at the picture, not by reasoning**: the grouping had the level constraint and the loader predicate did not follow it, and nothing but the rendered frame said so — which is the norm's own argument for the eye as an instrument ( #norm-no-depiction-without-referent FE(2)) turned on the instrument itself.

    Built as `watch::erosion_cohorts` (grouping; the never-merge properties convicted by `watch::tests::{settle_histories_are_grouped_by_source_tree_and_never_merged, a_coarse_sweep_and_a_fine_beacon_are_two_chains_not_one}`) and consumed by `vivarium explore`'s settle-history lens.

    Three corollaries the first instance made concrete. A cohort may be **ragged** — stages covering unequal tile counts — in which case a frame is one world-moment only where its stage reaches, and the reader reports the raggedness rather than presenting a partial moment as a whole one. A cohort that is *not* the running binary's is still a faithful history of the world that produced it; refusing to show it is not more honest than showing it under its own name, provided the name is on screen. And a fine cohort's **extent is part of its census**, not something a viewer can be expected to find: a beacon chain covers ~10⁻³ of the globe by area, and a reader that cannot say *where* its chain is holds data nobody can reach.

12. **Out of bounds for this segment.** Claiming any $\varepsilon$-gated kernel is built; specifying the store schema for a recorded $\varepsilon$; treating the time-index as an ordering over wall-clock rather than over world-time — a reader that orders by *landing* time is reading build history and must say so (FE(5)); and prescribing how a *view* should render a chain, which is #norm-no-depiction-without-referent 's and #form-core-view-wall 's business, not this segment's.

## Epistemic Status

**Max attainable: exact** as architecture, once a stage records an $\varepsilon$ and something can fail when it does not.

**Currently `robust-qualitative`.** The formulation is a 2026-07-10 design (`framework-to-status-quo` §4, component **E**) whose retention half is primary-read numerics (Gear–Wells 1984), and whose necessity is attested from four independent live surfaces — three `ASSUMPTIONS.md` **arbitrary** rows, two `NomosDecl` `status` strings, a `LEXICON.udon` note on derived epistemic state, and `#disc-unlawfulness-budget`'s "not claimed" clause. **No Joseph DECISIONS row**; do not cite as ratified.

**Built:** time-index in key, store residency, and resumability, on the mantle-thermal chain — convicted by `query::tests::{epoch_ladder_is_order_independent, resume_equals_run_through}` — and now on the **erosion chain**, the first materialized-only instance (FE(8)): interior stages as citizens at the manifest's `erosion_stage_stride`, chained ≡ one-shot bit-exact and the endpoint-only migration path, convicted by `query::tests::{staged_chain_is_bit_identical_to_one_shot, endpoint_only_world_gains_its_interior_and_readers_see_one_surface}`; recorded per-stage residual with reader (FE(3) first instance); surface readers dedupe to one region per tile — the latest accepted stage — so a surface is one moment, never a blend of moments. The **poller** of FE(5) (`vivarium watch`, live and `--replay` through one path). The **nested densification** of FE(9), with bit-exactness pinned by `mantle_thermal::tests::{canonical_chain_is_bit_identical_to_the_authored_literals, every_refinement_contains_every_coarser_one_exactly}` and the reuse measured at the CLI. The **interior census** of FE(2) (`watch::interior`), which is what moved that clause from architecture to measurement — and which measured the erosion interior landing (L9, 2026-07-28: 8 stages × 384 tiles in a 12.4 s sweep). The **world-time reading** of FE(11), in `vivarium explore`'s settle-history lens: cohort grouping in `watch::erosion_cohorts` (three properties convicted — never-merge across source trees, endpoint-only cohorts sorting behind real chains, raggedness reported), stage selection by `(src, epochs)` through the same `load_eroded_regions_where` the live and replay paths use, and per-stage recorded residuals read by cohort rather than through the current-source key. Measured on `first-light` (L9, 8 stages × 384 tiles): mean |Δh| against the uncarved prior runs 3.83 m at epoch 5 to 25.56 m at epoch 40, monotone; 88–91% of cells **rise** and 2.7–5.6% **fall**, so the settle history is uplift-dominated by area and the deepest incision reaches −866 m — which is what forced the view's change channel to be signed rather than a one-sided erosion ramp.

**Unbuilt:** any kernel running to a declared criterion rather than a count (the recorded erosion residual is data, not a gate); stage naming beyond a raw time-index; a world-time ordering for the *poller* — `vivarium watch --replay` still orders by root landing time, and since the builder is tile-major, a replayed erosion sweep shows a carving *wave* (build history), not the world evolving in step. The **3D explorer** now has the world-time reading (FE(12)); `watch` does not, and giving it one is the same key predicate over a census it already parses.

**Provenance worth keeping, because the failure recurs.** This component did not carry from its source plan into the six-phase build plan, and no deliberate deferral was ever recorded — the loss was noticed on 2026-07-10 and written down, and then the note recording it was itself archived. It survived only in instruments nobody reads for open work (an assumptions ledger, two status strings) and as a parenthetical in a segment about something else. Its absence had no `#gap` row until 2026-07-24, which is why the project's own open-problem census could not see it. Stage `draft`.

## Discussion

The four things most often asked of this project's runtime — build in the background toward somewhere, restart at the right point after a change, watch the edge live, watch a replay — look like four features and are one object plus one reader. All four need the same thing: **the interior of a build has to be addressable**. A time-index in the key is what makes it so, and a recorded $\varepsilon$ is what makes a stage's honesty travel with it instead of living in a comment.

Building the reader is what turned the last clause from a preference into a measurement. Live watching and replay came out as one path, as FE(5) predicted; what the reader then had to *report* was that almost nothing in the tree has an interior for it to read. That is not a defect in the reader, and it would not have been visible from the design: an instrument built to display a chain is also the instrument that counts how many chains exist.

The reason this matters beyond convenience: a build with no addressable interior cannot be *believed* either. "It converged" is a claim, and without the residual it reached, nothing can convict it ( #norm-declaration-must-convict ). The same construction that makes a build watchable makes it honest — which is not a coincidence, because both are the demand that a computation say where it actually got to.

## Working Notes

- **The first convicting step is not available on either live relaxation kernel, and both no-gos are measured.** Water: no stationarity to detect at the pinned timestep ( #obs-water-fill-never-settles ). Erosion: the residual is driver-bound — sustained uplift leaves no zero-residual equilibrium, and most tiles are subaerially empty and do no fluvial work at all ( #obs-erosion-residual-is-driver-bound ). A tolerance on $\lvert\Delta h\rvert$ fires wrongly or never on both. FE(4) stands as stated — *a criterion replaces a count* — but the criteria these two kernels need are a **step-size resolution** and an **erosion–uplift balance** respectively, and neither is a near-stationarity gate. What is available now is a **precondition** rather than a tolerance: a tile with no subaerial cells is converged before it starts.
- **The two "epochs" are separated, and `LEXICON` decided which sense keeps the word:** `epoch` is reserved *exclusively* for the erosion solver's step unit, so the cooling chain took this segment's own noun and is now `mantle_thermal::cooling_stages`. The CLI's `--epochs` is the erosion sense and is correctly named; `--frames` sets cooling-stage density.
- **The $\varepsilon$ home is provisionally settled as a sibling keyed artifact** (`aspect=stage-residual`, one f32 — the `epoch-reduction` aspect precedent), chosen over widening the stage memo so pre-chain readers keep decoding stage bytes as a bare height field. Revisit when a second kernel records one; do not harden further before then ( #form-store-as-save FE(3) reserves the shape).
- **Replay pacing vs world-time:** the builder is tile-major (a tile runs its whole ladder before the next tile starts), so landing-order replay shows carving sweep across faces. Stage-major scheduling, or an epoch-ordered reader, would each give world-time-coherent playback; the reader is the cheaper and does not touch the builder. Neither is built.
- Sibling gaps: the operated-instrument section and the demand spool (OUTLINE §III); `watchpoint` has no `LEXICON` entry while `beacon` and `focus` do.
