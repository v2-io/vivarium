# Every builder tile is carved as if surrounded by sea, so its incision driver is starved and its seams manufacture pits

The partial-tile outlet policy makes every edge cell a sink, so each tile grades itself to its own perimeter. **Measured: no tile-local drainage basin can exceed its tile — assembled routing of the L13 beacon patch finds a 3.25× larger trunk — and the seam cost is grain-dependent: read through an honest (wall-contract) reader, 63.5% of the beacon window sits in closed depressions and the two contracts carve beds 259 m apart on the tile rings, while at L9 planet grain the contracts agree within 10% (the 19.67% of FE(5) is an edge-sink reader's understatement of the same quantity).**

## Formal Expression

1. **The policy.** `Fluvial::outlets` marks a cell an outlet if it is at or below derived sea **or** — for any tile that is not a whole cube face — if it lies on the tile edge. Every builder tile at L9 (64² of a 512² face) and every beacon tile at L13 is such a partial tile. Priority-Flood then fills to those outlets each epoch, and stream-power incises toward them. The tile is therefore carved against a base level that is an artefact of the tiling, everywhere within $\le 32$ cells of any point.

2. **Measured: a tile-local read of the stored bed holds almost no closed depression.** Recomputing the depression fill on the *stored* L9 surfaces, tile by tile (`examples/discharge_probe` Part 7, live world `first-light`, 384 tiles): 96 tiles carry land, 65 869 subaerial cells in total, and **8 tiles** contain **23 cells** whose fill depth exceeds 1 m, deepest 367 m. Depression capacity is a per-cell quantity here — $\text{filled }h - \text{stored }h$ — not an aggregate that could hide a distribution.

   **The reader shares the carve's contract, so this number is bounded twice by the same policy.** Every edge cell of the tile is an outlet to the reader exactly as it was to the carve, so a basin reaching a tile edge is drained on the way in and invisible on the way out. What the number therefore measures is *basins wholly interior to a 64-cell tile*, which at 1251 km per tile is a narrow question.

3. **This policy is what remains between the bed and its basins, and standing water is still blocked twice over.** The per-epoch depression fill is no longer part of the answer: `Fluvial::erode` restores its raise, so a closed basin survives the carve and a whole-face L9 control retains 0.47–0.51 % of its land in one ( #obs-lakes-are-routed-over-not-carved-away FE(3)). What this policy contributes is two things at once — it **removes** inherited basins by grading each tile to its perimeter, and it **manufactures** FE(5)'s seam pits where adjacent tiles grade to different perimeter heights — and the grain sweep separates them: depressions still fall monotonically as the grain grows, from 571 cells at grain 8 to 134 at the whole face, so the excess at fine grain is seam and the whole-face floor is geography ( #obs-lakes-are-routed-over-not-carved-away FE(7)). Downstream, `water-tile` settles a conserved shallow-water depth on this bed and needs a closed depression to occupy, and #obs-water-fill-never-settles shows the fill buys 40 s of world time at any level — so a bed *with* depressions still would not fill them. Two independent sufficient causes remain; fixing either alone changes nothing.

4. **Measured: no basin can exceed its tile, and the cost is a factor of three.** On the L13 beacon patch (f1, origin (640, 5376), 4×4 tiles of 64², 30 stored stages) the largest tile-local trunk drains $3.58\times10^{9}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Re-routing the **assembled** 256² patch — the same stored heights, one field, tile-interior edges no longer sinks — gives $1.163\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, a **3.25×** larger trunk. Per tile, the largest basin collects 18.1–70.7% of the tile's runoff and 1–4 basins cover half of it; over the assembled patch the largest collects 14.9% and 6 basins cover half. The network is well integrated *at the scale it is permitted to be*, and that scale is 78 km.

5. **Measured: assembling the tiles manufactures depressions at the seams.** The same assembled patch that had **zero** depression cells tile-locally has **12 893 of 65 536** (19.67%) once the artificial edge sinks are removed, deepest 322 m, capacity $1.30\times10^{12}\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Each tile was graded to its own perimeter; adjacent tiles graded to different perimeter heights; the mismatch is a closed basin. So the depression statistic on an assembled surface is **not evidence of geography** — it is at least partly a seam artefact of the tiling, and the two cannot be separated by this measurement. (The grain sweep can, and does: #obs-lakes-are-routed-over-not-carved-away FE(7).) Both numbers are real and neither alone is the world. This is #form-seam-flux-exchange 's argument arriving in the hydrology: tiles that exchange *states* at a seam rather than *fluxes* disagree about where downhill is.

   **The 19.67% understates it, because that reader had edge sinks of its own.** A $256^2$ window at L13 is a partial tile, so the assembled re-route was itself performed against a perimeter ring of sinks. Read instead through a declared `NoFluxWall` reader — possible only since the contract became a parameter — the same footprint carved as sixteen tiles holds **41 588 of 65 536 cells (63.5%)** in closed depressions, capacity $1.16\times10^{13}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, and it holds them throughout: 40 548 cells at 50 epochs, 41 588 at 300. Carved instead as one $256^2$ field under that same contract, the count **decays from 11 301 at 50 epochs to 1 at 300** — the window has no coast, so its only outlet under that contract is its own lowest cell, and 300 epochs is enough to grade 313 km of fully subaerial ground to it. The two arms therefore differ by three orders of magnitude at every rung, and the tiled arm's count does not decay at all.

   **At this grain the contract restructures the whole window, not just its edge.** The two carves above end $203$–$259\,\mathrm{m}$ apart in mean $\lvert\Delta h\rvert$ after 300 epochs, rising monotonically from $56$–$63\,\mathrm{m}$ at 50 — and the gap is **not** concentrated at the boundary: 259.2 m on the tile perimeter rings against 203.5 m inside them. This is the regime the cross-tile repair is justified by, and it is the opposite of what the same comparison shows at the L9 planet build, where the two contracts agree within 5 % at every rung of a 300-epoch history ( #obs-lakes-are-routed-over-not-carved-away FE(8) and `examples/base_level_probe` Part 6). The difference is the grain against the geography: 78 km tiles here, 1251 km there.

   **Neither contract is *right* at this footprint, and that is the finding rather than a caveat.** The window is fully subaerial — 65 536 of 65 536 cells above derived sea — so `BaseLevelSink` surrounds it with an outlet ring that exists nowhere in the world, while `NoFluxWall` leaves it with no outlet at all and falls back to making its single lowest cell one. A patch with no coast has no honest base level that is a function of the patch. Only a boundary that exchanges with the neighbours it actually has can supply one, which is #form-seam-flux-exchange 's claim reaching the place where it is load-bearing.

6. **Measured: the settle history does not integrate the network.** Across all 30 stored stages of one beacon tile (epochs 10 → 300, one full a-priori response time), largest-basin share oscillates between 32.0% and 45.8% with no trend, channel-cell counts hold at ≈1000 / ≈180 / ≈17 at the three thresholds, and discharge-weighted MFD out-degree stays at 1.87–2.12. **Under the sink policy at this fine grain, the network is set within the first ten epochs and only jitters afterwards** — a conditional fact, not kernel law: at L9 the same policy grows the trunk $4.2\times$ over the same 300 epochs, through three visible reorganization events, and freeze-versus-capture under the wall contract has no stored history yet. Under FE(4) the beacon case is expected rather than surprising — base level is fixed 32 cells away in every direction, so there is no room for capture or basin growth — but it means the beacon's 300 epochs buy incision *depth*, not network *evolution*, and a scrub of the stage chain should not be described as watching drainage organize.

7. **The flow field is not a channel thread, measured on live terrain.** MFD at $p=1.0$ spreads to every downhill neighbour ( #obs-cube-locked-kernel-bias FE(1)); discharge-weighted out-degree is **1.65–2.27** across all 16 beacon tiles and 1.86 on the assembled patch — flow leaves a typical cell in two directions, not one. Peak concentration is nonetheless comparable to a single-receiver tree: $\max D8 / \max \mathrm{MFD}$ runs **0.47–1.01**, so the fan spreads *along the way* and re-converges at the trunk rather than smearing the trunk itself. A view painting MFD as a one-cell-wide river is therefore drawing a two-cell fan thin, which #norm-no-depiction-without-referent forbids; a view painting D8 instead is drawing a tree the kernel does not erode with.

8. **The straight runs are a fill artefact, not a router artefact, and only on assembled surfaces.** Longest run of identical D8 flow direction within a tile: **9–19 links**, median 1–2, with **0%** of long-run cells inside filled ground. On the assembled patch: max 51, 2 165 cells on runs $\ge 8$, and **83% of them inside filled ground** — the Priority-Flood $\varepsilon$-gradient orienting flow across the flats that FE(5)'s seam basins and the bed's own retained basins present to the reader.

   **This survives the fill repair, and the survival was predicted wrongly.** The $\varepsilon$'s *mass* term is retired — the epoch loop restores its raise, so no manufactured rock reaches the bed ( #obs-lakes-are-routed-over-not-carved-away FE(5)). Its *directional* term was predicted before re-running to persist at reduced amplitude; it persists at **increased** amplitude (1 740 → 2 165 long-run cells, 80% → 83% in fill), because there is now more filled ground for the reader to orient flow across. The prediction is recorded as a miss ( #norm-caught-disciplines-become-mechanisms FE(4)); what it got right is that restoring the raise does not remove the artefact, because incision is still applied where the $\varepsilon$ sent the flow.

## Epistemic Status

**Max attainable: exact** for the quoted measurements under `examples/discharge_probe` — deterministic, read-only, over the stored surfaces of one world (`first-light`, seed 17425063241017297386). Every clause is independently falsifiable: a tile-local read finding basins in quantity refutes FE(2); an assembled trunk no larger than a tile-local one refutes FE(4); a trending largest-basin share refutes FE(6); an out-degree of 1 refutes FE(7); long runs outside filled ground refute FE(8).

**Currently `exact` as observation.** The store read was **stale-by-`src`** for the binary that measured it (the probe says so on screen and the reason it is admissible is stated there): these are claims about the *shape* of surfaces the store really holds, and a surface carved under a previous source tree is honest terrain for a shape question. Re-running after a rebuild is expected to move the digits and not the clauses; if it moves a clause, that is a finding and this segment returns to `draft`.

**Not measured, and therefore not claimed:** whether any of this changes at L6 or L21. The **remedy** is likewise not claimed: halo-exchanged or flux-BC tile boundaries ( #form-seam-flux-exchange , #form-face-flux-register ) are the named direction, and neither is built or costed here — and #obs-lakes-are-routed-over-not-carved-away FE(11) measures that this policy is now the *remaining* obstacle to basins in the shipped tiling, rather than one of two.

**The whole-face control has been run** ( #obs-lakes-are-routed-over-not-carved-away FE(7)), and it separates the two populations this policy acts on: depressions still fall monotonically as the grain grows, so the excess at fine grain is seam-manufactured, while the whole-face floor of 134–228 cells is inherited geography that survives the carve. FE(2)'s 23 cells are neither of those — they are what a *tile-local reader* can see through this same policy's outlet ring. **The beacon has since been carved under both named contracts at its own level** (`EdgeContract` + `examples/beacon_contract_probe`): the tiled edge-sink carve and the whole-window wall carve end **259 m apart** on the tile rings with **63.5 %** of the tiled window in closed depressions against a single cell — so FE(4)'s starvation is convicted at its own grain, and the grain dependence is measured on both sides (at L9 the two contracts track within 10 %; a 1251 km tile never reaches its edges). **What remains unrun, and is the honest gap:** FE(6)'s network freeze across a settle *history* under the wall contract (the both-ways carves are endpoint comparisons; nobody has stored a 30-stage chain under `NoFluxWall`); any *keyed* selection of a contract — `EdgeContract` is a kernel parameter, deliberately outside the key until a keyed caller can choose ( #form-declared-boundary-contract ); and any honest contract at a fully coastless window, where neither named policy is right (one invents an outlet ring, the other a single outlet) and the answer is a halo that exchanges with real neighbours.

**Probe sensitivity** ( #norm-probe-sensitivity ): the depression measure is convicted in both directions by unit test — it reports zero on a tile graded to its outlets and fires on a constructed 200 m crater. Without the second half, "no depressions anywhere" would be indistinguishable from a measure that never fires.

Stage `draft`.

## Discussion

The policy is not a mistake. `Fluvial::outlets` documents edge sinks as *"the principled incomplete-tile base level until flux-BC"*, and full-face tiles already refuse them because they carved 300 m trenches around every cube face. What was missing is the price. The price is a tile-local incision driver starved by $3.85\times$, a network the beacon's 300 epochs do not evolve (under the sink policy at that grain — FE(6)'s conditions travel with this phrase), and — the moment anyone assembles tiles for a view — 13% of the surface in seam-manufactured pits with straight drainage crawling across them, a fraction that grows as the grain is made finer.

It also relocates a question, and then a control relocated it again. "Why is there no inland standing water?" reads as a water-kernel question and was filed as one; it is a question about the bed, and this segment's policy was the bed's most conspicuous artefact. Carving the world under both contracts moved it once more, to the fill inside the epoch loop; repairing the fill moved it back, with a number attached ( #obs-lakes-are-routed-over-not-carved-away ). That is the ordinary shape of #norm-probes-before-claims three times over: the instrument built to look at one system convicts the system underneath it, the control built to confirm a cause finds a different one, and repairing that cause promotes the original suspect to load-bearing on the evidence rather than the expectation.

## Working Notes

- **The grain dependence now convicts (2026-07-30).** `the_contract_disagreement_is_grain_dependent` asserts the two contracts disagree several-fold more at L13 than at L9, so quoting a fine-grain contract number as planet law now trips a test rather than only a reader. Measured while writing it, 64² each, same origin and epochs: **L13 26.4 m, L11 9.7 m, L9 7.0 m** mean $\lvert\Delta h\rvert$. This closes audit finding Q7 (Grok, 2026-07-28), which named the gap as "no self-firing guard" and a conversion target for #norm-caught-disciplines-become-mechanisms .
- **And the dependence is on *grain*, not on window extent — the intuitive version is false.** At fixed L13 the disagreement is **not** monotone in window size: 32/64/96 cells give 38.3 / 26.4 / 35.5 m. So "a smaller window suffers more edge effect" does not hold here, and a guard written from that intuition would have asserted the wrong relation. Measured incidentally while building the Q7 guard; one seed, one origin, so a caution against the intuition rather than a law about extent.


- **The seam step is this policy's cleanest signature, and it matures.** Mean $\lvert\Delta h\rvert$ across tile boundaries over mean $\lvert\Delta h\rvert$ elsewhere rises 0.04 above the uncarved prior's null at 40 epochs and **0.20 above it at 300** ( #obs-lakes-are-routed-over-not-carved-away FE(8)). It is unmoved by the fill repair, which is what makes it this policy's own signature rather than a shared one.
- **Do not read FE(5)'s 13% as a lake inventory.** It is the assembled patch's geometric capacity to its spill points, seam artefacts included, with no evaporation, inflow, seepage or residence time in the account. A view that painted it as water would be inventing both the water and the basins.
- **Consumers:** `Fluvial::drainage_surface` is the reader all of this is measured through — a recompute, not a store citizen ( `DECISIONS[drainage-is-a-reader-of-a-stage-not-a-store-citizen]` ). `Fluvial::from_region` leaves `precip_weight` at ones, so any caller that does not supply climate is reading uniform-rain discharge; at the beacon's 313 km span that is worth $\lt 1\%$ (the fated jitter's features are ~1000 km, so it is nearly constant across a patch), but the gap widens with span and it is not a safe default to inherit.
- **Forward, with FE(6)'s mechanism now known not to generalize:** running the same 300 epochs at L9 under this same edge-sink policy grows the trunk $4.2\times$, and the whole-face control tracks it within 5% at every rung ( #obs-lakes-are-routed-over-not-carved-away FE(8)). So "base level 32 cells away leaves no room for capture" is not a general property of the policy; at L9, where a tile spans 1251 km rather than 78 km, the epoch count is what bounds integration. FE(6) may still be right about the beacon — its endpoint alternative has now been carved (`beacon_contract_probe`), but no settle *history* has been stored under the wall contract, so freeze-versus-capture across stages remains untested there.
