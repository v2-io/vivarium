# Every builder tile is carved as if surrounded by sea, so its incision driver is starved and its seams manufacture pits

The partial-tile outlet policy makes every edge cell a sink, so each tile grades itself to its own perimeter. **Measured: no tile-local drainage basin can exceed its tile — assembled routing of the L13 beacon patch finds a 3.85× larger trunk — and the seam cost is grain-dependent: read through an honest (wall-contract) reader, 60.6% of the beacon window sits in closed depressions and the two contracts carve beds 280 m apart there, while at L9 planet grain the contracts agree within 10% (the 13% figure of FE(5) was an edge-sink reader's understatement).**

## Formal Expression

1. **The policy.** `Fluvial::outlets` marks a cell an outlet if it is at or below derived sea **or** — for any tile that is not a whole cube face — if it lies on the tile edge. Every builder tile at L9 (64² of a 512² face) and every beacon tile at L13 is such a partial tile. Priority-Flood then fills to those outlets each epoch, and stream-power incises toward them. The tile is therefore carved against a base level that is an artefact of the tiling, everywhere within $\le 32$ cells of any point.

2. **Measured: the eroded bed holds essentially no closed depression.** Recomputing the depression fill on the *stored* L9 surfaces (`examples/discharge_probe` Part 7, live world `first-light`, 384 tiles): 96 tiles carry land, 58 013 subaerial cells in total, and **3 tiles** contain **12 cells** whose fill depth exceeds 1 m. Depression capacity is a per-cell quantity here — $\text{filled }h - \text{stored }h$ — not an aggregate that could hide a distribution.

3. **The cause of that absence is the per-epoch fill, and standing water is blocked twice over — by neither this policy.** `Fluvial::erode` raises every closed depression to its spill point and keeps the raise, so the domain is depression-free by construction at the instant step (2) of the composition returns ( #obs-fill-writes-itself-into-the-bed ). Carving one face under **both** boundary contracts settles the attribution in the direction opposite to the expectation: a whole-face coast-only carve holds *fewer* closed depressions than any tiled carve of the same geography, and the count falls monotonically as the tile grain grows. What this policy contributes to the depression census is FE(5)'s seam pits — manufactured, not preserved — and the 12 cells above are one sample of a small population that deposition and creep re-create after each fill and the next fill erases. Downstream, `water-tile` settles a conserved shallow-water depth on this bed and needs a closed depression to occupy, so the second block compounds: #obs-water-fill-never-settles shows the fill buys 40 s of world time at any level, and even a bed *with* depressions would not have filled them. Two independent sufficient causes; fixing either alone changes nothing.

4. **Measured: no basin can exceed its tile, and the cost is a factor of four.** On the L13 beacon patch (f1, origin (640, 5376), 4×4 tiles of 64², 30 stored stages) the largest tile-local trunk drains $3.58\times10^{9}\,\mathrm{m^2}$. Re-routing the **assembled** 256² patch — the same stored heights, one field, tile-interior edges no longer sinks — gives $1.38\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{m^2}$, a **3.85×** larger trunk. Per tile, the largest basin collects 18–71% of the tile's runoff and 1–5 basins cover half of it; over the assembled patch the largest collects 17.5% and 7 basins cover half. The network is well integrated *at the scale it is permitted to be*, and that scale is 78 km.

5. **Measured: assembling the tiles manufactures depressions at the seams.** The same assembled patch that had **zero** depression cells tile-locally has **8 532 of 65 536** (13.0%) once the artificial edge sinks are removed, deepest 411 m, capacity $8.7\times10^{11}\,\mathrm{m^3}$. Each tile was graded to its own perimeter; adjacent tiles graded to different perimeter heights; the mismatch is a closed basin. So the depression statistic on an assembled surface is **not evidence of geography** — it is at least partly a seam artefact of the tiling, and the two cannot be separated by this measurement. Both numbers are real and neither alone is the world. This is #form-seam-flux-exchange 's argument arriving in the hydrology: tiles that exchange *states* at a seam rather than *fluxes* disagree about where downhill is.

   **The 13.0% understates it, because that reader had edge sinks of its own.** A $256^2$ window at L13 is a partial tile, so the assembled re-route was itself performed against a perimeter ring of sinks. Read instead through a declared `NoFluxWall` reader — possible only since the contract became a parameter — the same footprint carved as sixteen tiles holds **39 688 of 65 536 cells (60.6%)** in closed depressions, capacity $1.10\times10^{13}\,\mathrm{m^3}$, and it holds them throughout: 40 216 cells at 50 epochs, 39 688 at 300. Carved instead as one $256^2$ field under that same contract, the count is **0 to 15 cells** at every rung of the same history.

   **At this grain the contract restructures the whole window, not just its edge.** The two carves above end $274$–$297\,\mathrm{m}$ apart in mean $\lvert\Delta h\rvert$ after 300 epochs, rising monotonically from $\approx 200\,\mathrm{m}$ at 50 — and the gap is **not** concentrated at the boundary: 296.6 m on the tile perimeter rings against 273.8 m inside them. This is the regime the cross-tile repair is justified by, and it is the opposite of what the same comparison shows at the L9 planet build, where the two contracts agree within 10 % at every rung ( #obs-fill-writes-itself-into-the-bed FE(7)). The difference is the grain against the geography: 78 km tiles here, 1251 km there.

   **Neither contract is *right* at this footprint, and that is the finding rather than a caveat.** The window is fully subaerial — 65 536 of 65 536 cells above derived sea — so `BaseLevelSink` surrounds it with an outlet ring that exists nowhere in the world, while `NoFluxWall` leaves it with no outlet at all and falls back to making its single lowest cell one. A patch with no coast has no honest base level that is a function of the patch. Only a boundary that exchanges with the neighbours it actually has can supply one, which is #form-seam-flux-exchange 's claim reaching the place where it is load-bearing.

6. **Measured: the settle history does not integrate the network.** Across all 30 stored stages of one beacon tile (epochs 10 → 300, one full a-priori response time), largest-basin share oscillates between 32.6% and 45.8% with no trend, channel-cell counts hold at ≈1000 / ≈185 / ≈17 at the three thresholds, and discharge-weighted MFD out-degree stays at 1.96–2.09. **Under the sink policy at this fine grain, the network is set within the first ten epochs and only jitters afterwards** — a conditional fact, not kernel law: at L9 the same policy grows the trunk 2.4× over the same 300 epochs ( #obs-fill-writes-itself-into-the-bed FE(7)), and freeze-versus-capture under the wall contract has no stored history yet. Under FE(4) the beacon case is expected rather than surprising — base level is fixed 32 cells away in every direction, so there is no room for capture or basin growth — but it means the beacon's 300 epochs buy incision *depth*, not network *evolution*, and a scrub of the stage chain should not be described as watching drainage organize.

7. **The flow field is not a channel thread, measured on live terrain.** MFD at $p=1.0$ spreads to every downhill neighbour ( #obs-cube-locked-kernel-bias FE(1)); discharge-weighted out-degree is **1.96–2.30** across all 16 beacon tiles and 2.00 on the assembled patch — flow leaves a typical cell in two directions, not one. Peak concentration is nonetheless comparable to a single-receiver tree: $\max D8 / \max \mathrm{MFD}$ runs **0.56–1.01**, so the fan spreads *along the way* and re-converges at the trunk rather than smearing the trunk itself. A view painting MFD as a one-cell-wide river is therefore drawing a two-cell fan thin, which #norm-no-depiction-without-referent forbids; a view painting D8 instead is drawing a tree the kernel does not erode with.

8. **The straight runs are a fill artefact, not a router artefact, and only on assembled surfaces.** Longest run of identical D8 flow direction within a tile: **10–20 links**, median 2, with **0%** of long-run cells inside filled ground. On the assembled patch: max 56, 1 740 cells on runs $\ge 8$, and **80% of them inside filled ground** — the Priority-Flood $\varepsilon$-gradient orienting flow across the flats that FE(5)'s seam basins create. The residual table in #detail-erosion-composition names the $\varepsilon$-fill as a *mass* mint; it is also a **directional** one, and that is new here.

## Epistemic Status

**Max attainable: exact** for the quoted measurements under `examples/discharge_probe` — deterministic, read-only, over the stored surfaces of one world (`first-light`, seed 17425063241017297386). Every clause is independently falsifiable: a bed with closed depressions refutes FE(2); an assembled trunk no larger than a tile-local one refutes FE(4); a trending largest-basin share refutes FE(6); an out-degree of 1 refutes FE(7); long runs outside filled ground refute FE(8).

**Currently `exact` as observation.** The store read was **stale-by-`src`** for the binary that measured it (the probe says so on screen and the reason it is admissible is stated there): these are claims about the *shape* of surfaces the store really holds, and a surface carved under a previous source tree is honest terrain for a shape question. Re-running after a rebuild is expected to move the digits and not the clauses; if it moves a clause, that is a finding and this segment returns to `draft`.

**Not measured, and therefore not claimed:** whether any of this changes at L6 or L21. The **remedy** is likewise not claimed: halo-exchanged or flux-BC tile boundaries ( #form-seam-flux-exchange , #form-face-flux-register ) are the named direction, and neither is built or costed here — and #obs-fill-writes-itself-into-the-bed measures that repairing them will not, by itself, put standing water anywhere.

**The whole-face control has been run** ( #obs-fill-writes-itself-into-the-bed ), and it answered FE(2) and FE(5) in the direction opposite to the expectation: a whole-face coast-only carve holds *fewer* closed depressions than any tiled carve, so the 12 cells of FE(2) are neither geography nor evidence for this policy — they are deposition pits in a small population that the next epoch's fill erases. That is why FE(3) now attributes the absence to the fill. **The beacon has since been carved under both named contracts at its own level** (`EdgeContract` + `examples/beacon_contract_probe`): the tiled edge-sink carve and the whole-window wall carve end **280 m apart** with **60.6 %** of the tiled window in closed depressions against ~0 — so FE(4)'s starvation is convicted at its own grain, and the grain dependence is measured on both sides (at L9 the two contracts track within 10 %; a 1251 km tile never reaches its edges). **What remains unrun, and is the honest gap:** FE(6)'s network freeze across a settle *history* under the wall contract (the both-ways carves are endpoint comparisons; nobody has stored a 30-stage chain under `NoFluxWall`); any *keyed* selection of a contract — `EdgeContract` is a kernel parameter, deliberately outside the key until a keyed caller can choose ( #form-declared-boundary-contract ); and any honest contract at a fully coastless window, where neither named policy is right (one invents an outlet ring, the other a single outlet) and the answer is a halo that exchanges with real neighbours.

**Probe sensitivity** ( #norm-probe-sensitivity ): the depression measure is convicted in both directions by unit test — it reports zero on a tile graded to its outlets and fires on a constructed 200 m crater. Without the second half, "no depressions anywhere" would be indistinguishable from a measure that never fires.

Stage `draft`.

## Discussion

The policy is not a mistake. `Fluvial::outlets` documents edge sinks as *"the principled incomplete-tile base level until flux-BC"*, and full-face tiles already refuse them because they carved 300 m trenches around every cube face. What was missing is the price. The price is a tile-local incision driver starved by $3.85\times$, a network the beacon's 300 epochs do not evolve (under the sink policy at that grain — FE(6)'s conditions travel with this phrase), and — the moment anyone assembles tiles for a view — 13% of the surface in seam-manufactured pits with straight drainage crawling across them, a fraction that grows as the grain is made finer.

It also relocates a question, and then a control relocated it again. "Why is there no inland standing water?" reads as a water-kernel question and was filed as one; it is a question about the bed, and this segment's policy was the bed's most conspicuous artefact. Carving the world under both contracts moved it once more, to the fill inside the epoch loop ( #obs-fill-writes-itself-into-the-bed ). That is the ordinary shape of #norm-probes-before-claims twice over: the instrument built to look at one system convicts the system underneath it, and the control built to confirm a cause finds a different one.

## Working Notes

- **The seam step is this policy's cleanest signature, and it matures.** Mean $\lvert\Delta h\rvert$ across tile boundaries over mean $\lvert\Delta h\rvert$ elsewhere rises 0.03–0.05 above the uncarved prior's null at 40 epochs and **0.21 above it at 300** ( #obs-fill-writes-itself-into-the-bed FE(8)). Where the depression and trunk statistics are confounded by the fill, this one is not.
- **Do not read FE(5)'s 13% as a lake inventory.** It is the assembled patch's geometric capacity to its spill points, seam artefacts included, with no evaporation, inflow, seepage or residence time in the account. A view that painted it as water would be inventing both the water and the basins.
- **Consumers:** `Fluvial::drainage_surface` is the reader all of this is measured through — a recompute, not a store citizen ( `DECISIONS[drainage-is-a-reader-of-a-stage-not-a-store-citizen]` ). `Fluvial::from_region` leaves `precip_weight` at ones, so any caller that does not supply climate is reading uniform-rain discharge; at the beacon's 313 km span that is worth $\lt 1\%$ (the fated jitter's features are ~1000 km, so it is nearly constant across a patch), but the gap widens with span and it is not a safe default to inherit.
- **Forward, with FE(6)'s mechanism now known not to generalize:** running the same 300 epochs at L9 under this same edge-sink policy grows the trunk $2.4\times$, and the whole-face control tracks it within 10% at every rung ( #obs-fill-writes-itself-into-the-bed FE(7)). So "base level 32 cells away leaves no room for capture" is not a general property of the policy; at L9, where a tile spans 1251 km rather than 78 km, the epoch count is what bounds integration. FE(6) may still be right about the beacon — its endpoint alternative has now been carved (`beacon_contract_probe`), but no settle *history* has been stored under the wall contract, so freeze-versus-capture across stages remains untested there.
