## Corrections made, by defect

(2003) colour/motion detection table (01 §2.1, and mirrored in `appendix/cross-run-synthesis-2026-07-31.md` §1(a)): corrected 6%/25%/<2% to 5.5%/24%/~0%. **Re-extracted the PDF myself this pass** (`pdftotext -layout` on a fresh fetch) rather than trusting the audit's number. Finding beyond what either audit reported: **both figures are real, verbatim quotes from the same primary** — the paper's §4.2.1 Results (p.524-525) gives 5.5%/24%/"nearly a 0%", and its own p.541 General Discussion later paraphrases the same result as "6% to 25%"/"less than 2%". This was not a dossier transcription error or a rounding choice; it was two different sentences in one source, and the dossier had quoted the looser one. Both audits called it "rounding," which undersells what's actually going on — worth stating precisely rather than accepting the audit's framing wholesale.

## Correction beyond what either audit reported: the Bartram frequency claim

01 §2.3 said "Frequencies across the whole tested 1–3 Hz range were effective." Audit 1 flagged this as conflating a tested factorial range with a design-recommendation envelope, but didn't fully resolve it. I re-extracted the PDF and found the actual shape: the F(7,84)/F(1,12) experiment (§6.1.4, the one behind 01 §2.2's statistics) tested **exactly two discrete frequency levels** — SLOW (~1 Hz) and FAST (~2 Hz) — not a swept range. The "1–3 Hz" language is the paper's *own* guideline box (G4), which appears to fold in a third, unrelated ~3 Hz value used only in a *different* experiment's amplitude manipulation (§4.1.1.6). That's an internal inconsistency in the primary itself, not a dossier gloss error — and it's a more precise diagnosis than either audit reached, because neither re-extracted the full methods section against the guideline box. Documented in 01 §2.3 with both quotes shown side by side.

## The cross-run synthesis scope-fence violation

Both audits independently flagged the same thing as their highest- or near-highest-severity finding: `appendix/cross-run-synthesis-2026-07-31.md` argues the CARVE red chip is "close to the worst available choice," while `00-INDEX.md` §5 claims the dossier "makes no claims about vivarium, proposes no changes." I judged this a cheap, clearly-right fix rather than a "reshape what the dossier is" call, because both audits converged on the same diagnosis and the fix doesn't delete any real evidence — it separates tiers that had been compounded:

- Added a banner at the top of the cross-run file and reworked §1 to break the four "independent lines" into their actual evidence tiers — Bartram (real, now-corrected numbers, but tested expected cues on a static field), red-on-black APCA (conditional on a non-adopted contrast model), Suchow & Alvarez motion-silencing (extended by analogy, not measured for this geometry), and Nikolic (identity/abstract only, and about a *green* box, not specifically red).
- Rewrote the "close to the worst available choice" verdict as an explicit correction: the evidence supports "colour-only peripheral status indication is a poor bet under several independent literatures; the vivarium-specific geometry is unmeasured; red is not specially convicted over other colours" — narrower and more honest than the compound verdict.
- Updated 00-INDEX §5 to state the cross-run is the one file that argues a design position, rather than letting "the appendix is closer to the evidence" apply uniformly.

I did not split the cross-run into a separate `IMPLICATIONS.md` file (both audits' heavier recommendation). That's a real structural option and I don't think it's clearly better than banner-plus-hedge — it would cost the file's usefulness as a single readable brief for a marginal truth gain now that the tier-mixing is named at the point of the verdict. Flagging this as a judgment call rather than a settled one.

## Where I think the audits are imprecise, stated plainly

- Both audits characterize the Bartram 6%/25%/<2% figures as the dossier "rounding" a primary number. That's not what happened — see above. The dossier quoted a real sentence from the wrong section of the same paper.
- Audit 1's frequency-range finding (5.2) is correct in outcome but underspecified in mechanism; it doesn't identify that the "1–3 Hz" language comes from the paper's own guideline box, or that this looks like the guideline box folding in an unrelated experiment's fixed frequency. Neither audit re-extracted the full methods text against the guideline box to check this.
- I found no place where I think an audit is simply wrong. Both are unusually careful, and where they disagree with each other (the egui hazard) I don't think either is obviously right, which is why it's left open rather than adjudicated by me.

## The retraction question

Joseph's brief explicitly raised this as a live option: was patching the right move, or should more have been retracted? My answer, after doing the repair: **retraction was not warranted, and I don't think it was close.**

Every defect either audit found was narrow and mechanically repairable — a wrong number, an unresolved-but-resolvable date, a mislabeled tag, a false claim about what a directory contains, a verdict that mixed evidence tiers without saying so. None of them indicted the dossier's underlying methodology or its 25-claim verified tier, which both audits independently spot-checked against primaries and found solid (audit 1: "one of the better LLM-mediated research dossiers I have seen on method"; audit 2: "unusually honest, usable research product"). The Bevy mechanism findings in particular — the text-remeasure cost, the FontSmoothing pixel-snap reversal, the viewport-origin correction — are hard-won, source-verified, and untouched by either audit's findings. Retracting the dossier to avoid twelve traceable, dated corrections would have destroyed real, checked research to duck a repair that turned out to be tractable. The one place a retraction-shaped move was genuinely on the table — the cross-run's design verdict — I chose hedge-and-reframe over deletion, because the underlying evidence lines are real; only the compound conclusion overstated them.
